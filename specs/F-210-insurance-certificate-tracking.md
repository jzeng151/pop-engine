# F-210 · Insurance Certificate Tracking

**Status:** PROPOSED (2026-07-26) — ready for review; not implementable until approved and listed in `docs/BASELINE.md`.

**Phase:** 3 · **Issue:** [#32](https://github.com/jzeng151/pop-engine/issues/32) · **Owner:** TBD · **Reviewer:** product owner · **Approval date:** —

## Purpose and User Outcome

An organizer can track policy, coverage, additional-insured, certificate status, and expiration while keeping rule-derived requirements separate from user-recorded evidence.

## Scope

**In scope**

- Create certificate records linked to the F-201 insurance plan items (R10/R11) and their F-202 checklist elements, and record policy reference, coverage, additional-insured text, status, expiration, and private file.
- Show gaps between the published requirement and organizer-confirmed certificate facts.
- Preserve history for replacement and expiration.

**Non-goals**

- Insurance sales, broker integration, legal sufficiency determination, OCR, or automatic agency acceptance.
- Changing the F-201 insurance findings or the F-205 card that surfaces them.

## Dependencies and Baseline

- F-201, F-205, F-202, F-209 controlled documents, and the F-701/F-702/F-703 gate. F-702 supplies the workspace membership boundary the certificate and its event resolve against and F-703 supplies the permission matrix `F210-AC-10` checks; F-701 supplies the authenticated actor both read from. F-701 is APPROVED (2026-07-28, `docs/BASELINE.md`); F-702 and F-703 remain PROPOSED, so the gate is not an approved input today and this spec is not implementable against it until those two are approved and listed in `docs/BASELINE.md`.
- Approved certificate status/money/date and upload contracts.
- Baseline at draft time: PRD, Roadmap, Design, and Phase 0–1.5 Architecture approved 2026-07-22; `ARCHITECTURE-FUTURE.md` approved as a planning target 2026-07-25; NYC ruleset `nyc.v2.7`, rules schema `popengine-rules/v2`, and scenario fixtures v5 where regulatory output is consumed.
- Operand binding and replay ordering for client-supplied identities: `specs/F-411-staff-roles-credentialed-entry.md` F411-AC-08 states once, for this whole branch, that a client-supplied identity is committed with the operands that determine its recorded result and that a reuse carrying different operands is a conflict rather than a replay, and that a committed identity is resolved before any version, generation, state, or limit check whose answer the committed operation itself changed, while the acting actor's current authority is re-read at the replay and must still admit the operation before any stored outcome is returned. Every identity criterion below relies on it. F-411 is PROPOSED, so that rule is not an approved input today and this spec is not implementable against it until F-411 is approved or the rule is promoted to an approved shared invariant; F411-AC-08 records both paths.
- The approval PR must re-pin any baseline version that changes before approval. A proposed or superseded input blocks implementation.

## Inputs, Outputs, State, Validation, and Errors

- Inputs are immutable insurance finding plus organizer-confirmed certificate facts; outputs are source-linked certificate versions.
- State is missing → recorded → expiring/expired or replaced; scan safety and organizer status remain separate.
- Unknown coverage or additional-insured text remains unknown and cannot satisfy a requirement automatically.
- Missing or unresolved material data stays visibly unset, unknown, pending, or failed as appropriate; it never becomes a successful or complete result.
- Invalid input produces a field or action-specific error without partial mutation. Retriable external failures preserve the user's confirmed state and expose a safe retry.

## UI and Accessibility

- Requirement and evidence appear side by side; warnings state what is missing without declaring legal invalidity.
- The complete workflow is keyboard operable, uses programmatic labels and visible focus, does not encode status by color alone, and announces asynchronous success or failure.
- Empty, loading, permission-denied, validation, provider-failure, and unavailable states have explicit copy and a safe next action.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **F210-AC-01:** A certificate version links to the exact insurance finding and preserves its policy reference, versioned certificate status, entered source, dates, coverage, additional-insured text, and file.
2. **F210-AC-02:** Missing or unknown coverage/additional-insured facts remain visibly unresolved and never auto-complete the requirement.
3. **F210-AC-03:** Replacement preserves the earlier version and follows F210-AC-08; expiration uses the approved timezone/date rule.
4. **F210-AC-04:** The UI never labels a certificate legally sufficient or agency accepted without an explicit authoritative record.
5. **F210-AC-05:** Unauthorized, unsafe, oversized, or mismatched files are unavailable and cannot create a recorded certificate version. The size and type bounds are the ones the upload policy named in the Approval Blockers establishes; until it is approved this criterion is testable only as "configured finite size and type bounds are enforced against the file's verified bytes and a file outside them is rejected," not against a specific number or type list.
6. **F210-AC-06:** A certificate links to the F-201 plan item that carries the insurance requirement (the R10 `insurance` item or the R11 `note` item) by that item's identity and version, and to the F-202 checklist element derived from it, never to F-205 state. Approved F-205 owns no finding of its own: its acceptance criteria consume the existing R10/R11 plan items from F-201, F205-AC-04 links its card to the R10 checklist item, and F205-AC-05 states that removing F-205 loses only the dedicated card while the insurance findings still appear in the plan from F-201. Pinning the finding to F-205 would therefore either invent a second record for one requirement or leave this projection following a card rather than the plan item it displays. When a new plan supersedes or removes that pinned F-201 item, the prior requirement-to-certificate projection becomes stale while certificate history remains intact; a current finding requires explicit handling and is never guessed or silently remapped to old evidence. Removing or disabling the F-205 card alone changes nothing here, because the pinned item survives it. The pinned F-201 plan item and the F-202 checklist element derived from it must resolve to the same workspace as the certificate that links to them, and to that certificate's event where the linked record is event-scoped, checked server-side inside the transaction that binds the link. F210-AC-10 admits the operation by the actor's current membership of the workspace owning the certificate, and this criterion requires only that the exact item identity and version be pinned; neither says where the pinned item lives, so a user holding a membership of two workspaces can create or replace a certificate in one while naming an F-201 insurance item and F-202 element from the other. Both checks pass, and the requirement-to-certificate projection F210-AC-06 defines then reports another event's insurance requirement as covered by this certificate. A link whose workspace or event does not match rejects the whole write, records no certificate version, appends no projection, and returns a response that does not distinguish a plan item or checklist element that does not exist from one in another workspace.
7. **F210-AC-07:** Every certificate stays in private storage; each download issuance rechecks the acting actor's current F-702 membership of the workspace that owns the certificate together with the F-703 certificate read and download permission approved for the action, both re-read server-side at that issuance on the same terms F210-AC-10 states for every other operation, and rechecks scan state, and returns only a short-lived signed URL. Membership is not the check on its own. A workspace member who holds no certificate read or download permission passes a membership-level recheck, so stating the recheck at that level hands them a signed URL for a private insurance certificate while F210-AC-10 is still refusing them the policy reference, coverage value, and additional-insured text on every read surface, and the file carries all of it. The permission on the parent record is not a permission on the file, and this criterion is what makes the two the same check. The issuance also compares the stored file to the certificate the request names, and that certificate to the workspace and, where the certificate is event-scoped, the event the actor was admitted for, checked server-side at the issuance before any URL is minted. Without that comparison the actor check is made against the right certificate while the URL is minted for another certificate's file: a member holding the download permission passes every actor-side test legitimately, and the file is a real stored object with a safe scan state, so nothing else in this criterion notices. A file that does not resolve to the named certificate yields no URL, and the refusal does not distinguish a file that does not exist from one this certificate may not download, on the same terms `F702-AC-10`'s fourth obligation states for every record an operation reaches through the one it names.

   A signed URL is a location, not an authority. Its bearer is never re-checked, so the only authorization it can ever carry is the one read at issuance, and any onward holder of the string reaches the certificate for its whole disclosed validity whatever their permission. That is why issuance is the moment the permission is read, why authorization loss blocks new URLs, and why an already issued direct-storage URL retains only its disclosed bounded validity until expiry rather than being revocable; the bounded lifetime is the whole of the mitigation, and shortening it does not turn possession into permission.

   F-703 is PROPOSED and names no role set, so the certificate read and download permission above cannot be named. Until F-703 is approved this criterion is testable only as "a download URL is issued only when the acting actor holds an active membership of the workspace that owns the named certificate, read server-side at that issuance, and the certificate's scan state is safe, and a refusal discloses nothing about whether that certificate exists", not against a named role or permission identifier. Naming that permission with F-703 is an approval blocker below.
8. **F210-AC-08:** A replacement names the exact certificate version it was composed against and commits only by compare-and-swap on that version: one transaction appends the new version and advances the current-certificate projection while the named version is still current. A replacement naming a superseded version is rejected, is not appended, and returns the current version for the organizer to resubmit against. Every replacement carries a stable request identity, committed with the appended version under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the certificate it replaces, and a request presenting an already committed identity is resolved from that record before the version comparison above and returns the outcome that request originally recorded, appending no second version. The ordering is stated because the two rules otherwise contradict each other on one request: when a replacement commits and its response is lost, the retry still names the certificate version the organizer read, which that commit superseded, so a comparison made first rejects it as stale for work that in fact succeeded and the organizer resubmits, appending a second version for one intended replacement. Concurrent replacements of one certificate therefore end with exactly one accepted current version and explicit rejections, never a last-write-wins projection that hides a confirmed update. "Replacement" here is every appended certificate version, not only a new file: a change to the policy reference, certificate status, entered source, dates, coverage, or additional-insured text AC-01 records is appended and compare-and-swapped on the same terms, so no field listed there can be edited without naming the certificate version it was composed against. That identity binds its operands under the rule `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch: it is committed together with the request fields that determine the recorded result, including every aggregate, version, and generation the request names, and a later request presenting the same identity with any different operand is refused as a conflict rather than being answered with the stored result. The identity is still the key and the operands are only a precondition on reusing it, so this is not the content uniqueness this criterion already forbids. That identity is resolved in the order `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch, applied here rather than restated: a request presenting an already committed identity is resolved from that record before the version, generation, state, and limit checks this criterion requires, and returns the outcome that request originally recorded, because the commit the retry repeats is exactly what moved the state those checks read. The acting actor's current authority is not among the checks the identity is resolved past: the authority this feature requires for a first attempt is re-read server-side at the replay, and the stored outcome is returned only if it still admits the operation, so a replay presented after that authority is gone is refused and discloses no more than a first request would; the only exception is authority the committed operation itself removed, which `F411-AC-08` states once for this branch. A request whose identity resolves to no committed outcome is then held to every one of them, and a request refused by one of them commits no identity, so re-presenting it later is a first attempt and not a replay.
9. **F210-AC-09:** Creating a certificate binds the request to a stable client-supplied request identity, committed with the first version under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the insurance finding it satisfies. A retry presenting the same identity returns the original certificate and its first version and appends nothing; a deliberately separate certificate uses a new identity. This is request identity, never content uniqueness: two genuinely distinct certificates carrying the same policy reference, dates, and coverage are both created, and a repeated identity is never rejected as a duplicate value. That identity binds its operands under the rule `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch: it is committed together with the request fields that determine the recorded result, including every aggregate, version, and generation the request names, and a later request presenting the same identity with any different operand is refused as a conflict rather than being answered with the stored result. The identity is still the key and the operands are only a precondition on reusing it, so this is not the content uniqueness this criterion already forbids. That identity is resolved in the order `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch, applied here rather than restated: a request presenting an already committed identity is resolved from that record before the version, generation, state, and limit checks this criterion requires, and returns the outcome that request originally recorded, because the commit the retry repeats is exactly what moved the state those checks read. The acting actor's current authority is not among the checks the identity is resolved past: the authority this feature requires for a first attempt is re-read server-side at the replay, and the stored outcome is returned only if it still admits the operation, so a replay presented after that authority is gone is refused and discloses no more than a first request would; the only exception is authority the committed operation itself removed, which `F411-AC-08` states once for this branch. A request whose identity resolves to no committed outcome is then held to every one of them, and a request refused by one of them commits no identity, so re-presenting it later is a first attempt and not a replay.

   A deliberate second certificate for the same finding is permitted and is not a replacement, so this criterion says what it means rather than leaving the projection AC-06 defines with two candidates. One finding may hold any number of certificates, each with its own version history under AC-08, and each is a separate record with a separate creation identity. Exactly one of them is the finding's current certificate at any moment, and it is the one the organizer has explicitly designated; creating the first certificate for a finding designates it, and creating a later one does not, so a deliberate second certificate is added beside the first without silently displacing it. Changing which certificate is current is its own designation action, which names the certificate it is moving from and the one it is moving to and compare-and-swaps the finding's current-certificate projection, so two concurrent designations cannot both win. Both named certificates must be held by the finding whose projection is moving, compared server-side inside that same transaction, and a designation naming a certificate of any other finding is refused: it moves the projection nowhere, appends no history, and returns a response that does not distinguish a certificate that does not exist from one another finding holds. The finding is the narrower aggregate the workspace comparison F210-AC-10 makes does not reach, which is what `F702-AC-10`'s fourth obligation requires when a spec's own aggregate is narrower than a workspace: the comparison is made at that scope as well and not instead. Without it the two certificates and the projection are checked only for currency and for workspace, and two certificates of two different findings in one workspace satisfy both. A designation can then point finding B at finding A's certificate, and AC-06's requirement-to-certificate projection and the requirement status derived from it read A's policy reference, dates, coverage, and additional-insured text as B's current evidence, so a requirement is reported satisfied by a certificate that was never filed against it while every workspace, version, and replay check passes. That action is a durable write of its own and carries its own stable client-supplied request identity, committed with the designation under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the finding whose projection it moves; the creation identity above and the replacement identity in AC-08 are scoped to a certificate and cover neither. A request presenting an already committed designation identity is resolved from that record before the projection comparison and returns the outcome that request originally recorded, moving the projection no second time. The ordering is stated for the same reason AC-08 states it: when a designation from A to B commits and its response is lost, the retry still names A as the certificate it is moving from, which that commit displaced, so a comparison made first reports a stale designation for work that in fact succeeded and the organizer is left reconciling a change that already applied. That identity binds its operands under the rule `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch: it is committed together with the finding, the certificate being moved from, and the certificate being moved to, and a later request presenting the same identity with any different operand is refused as a conflict rather than being answered with the stored result, so a reused identity can never move the projection to a certificate the original request did not name. AC-06's requirement-to-certificate projection and the requirement status derived from it read the designated current certificate and its current version, and never a set. The others remain visible as the finding's certificate history and are neither superseded versions of the current one nor a second answer to what the organizer holds.

   Designation rather than recency is what closes it, and the alternative was worse: making the newest certificate current would let a second upload silently replace evidence the organizer meant to keep beside it, which is the outcome AC-08 exists to prevent within one certificate. Making a second certificate an AC-08 replacement was the other option the review offered and is not taken here, because AC-08 replacement preserves a version chain of one certificate and two certificates for one finding are legitimately different documents, not two versions of one.

   AC-08 gives replacements this behaviour and starts at the second version, so the first one is the gap. When creation commits and its response is lost, the retry appends a second certificate record and a second private file reference for one upload. AC-01 makes both well-formed and AC-06 links both to the same finding, so nothing marks either superseded and the organizer's current evidence has two candidates with no rule to choose between them.

10. **F210-AC-10:** Every operation this feature defines names the certificate it acts on and the workspace that owns it, and is admitted only by the acting actor's current F-702 membership of that workspace together with the F-703 permission approved for the action, both re-read server-side from stored membership and role at the moment of the operation and, for a write, inside the same transaction that commits it. That covers certificate creation under F210-AC-09, replacement and every appended field edit under F210-AC-08, the current-certificate designation action under F210-AC-09, and every read: the certificate and version views under F210-AC-01, the gap and unresolved views under F210-AC-02, and the requirement-to-certificate projection reads under F210-AC-06. F210-AC-07 remains the rule for its leg, the recheck of the same current membership, the same F-703 certificate read and download permission, and scan state at each download issuance, and F210-AC-05 remains the file-safety rule rejecting unauthorized, unsafe, oversized, or mismatched files; this criterion does not restate either and gates everything they do not reach. A request failing the check is refused before any durable write and before any policy reference, coverage value, additional-insured text, status, date, or projection is disclosed, and its response does not distinguish a certificate that does not exist from one the actor may not see. The check is at the operation and not at session start or workspace switch, so authority removed while a request is in flight causes that request to fail rather than commit.

    Without this criterion AC-01 through AC-04, AC-06, AC-08, and AC-09 all pass for a caller who names another workspace's finding or certificate. They fix the linkage and preserved fields, the visibility of unknown coverage, the labeling rules, the projection pinning, the compare-and-swap and replay identity of replacements, and the creation identity and designation semantics, and not one of them asks who the actor is; AC-07 and AC-05 gate only download issuance and file content, on their own terms. The surface that set leaves open reads another organizer's policy references, coverage amounts, and certificate history, and appends versions, creates certificates, and moves the current-certificate designation in their workspace.

    One input this criterion needs is not established by any approved artifact today and is not invented here. F-703 is PROPOSED and names no role set, so the permission above cannot be named. Until F-703 is approved this criterion is testable only as "every certificate read and mutation is refused unless the acting actor holds an active membership of the workspace that owns the named certificate, read server-side at that operation, and a refusal discloses nothing about whether that certificate exists", not against a named role or permission identifier. Naming the certificate read and mutation permissions with F-703 is an approval blocker below.

## Fixtures and Verification

- F210-AC-09 includes a fixture in which a second certificate is deliberately created for one finding, the first stays designated current, AC-06's projection and the requirement status still read exactly one certificate, and a subsequent designation change moves it with two concurrent designations resolving to one winner.
- F210-AC-09 includes a cross-finding designation fixture in which two findings of one workspace each hold a certificate and a designation on finding B names finding A's certificate as the one it is moving to, with the projection generation and both certificate versions genuinely current: the designation is refused, B's projection does not move, AC-06's projection and requirement status for B disclose no policy reference, date, coverage, or additional-insured text from A's certificate, and the refusal does not distinguish a certificate that does not exist from one another finding holds. An equivalent fixture proves the same refusal when only the certificate being moved from belongs to the other finding, and a same-finding control fixture in which the identical designation commits.
- Planned automated fixture IDs are the acceptance IDs above; each must map one-to-one to a runnable test before approval can claim implementation readiness.
- Regulatory fixtures: Use the approved insurance-finding scenarios F-205 exercises (A, C, D, E), whose R10/R11 items come from F-201; all certificate data and files are synthetic.
- F210-AC-08 includes a fixture in which a replacement commits, its response is lost, and the retry presenting the same request identity and the pre-replacement certificate version returns the original recorded outcome rather than a superseded-version rejection, appending no second version.
- F210-AC-09 includes a designation fixture in which a move from certificate A to certificate B commits, its response is lost, and the retry presenting the same designation identity and A as the certificate it is moving from returns the original recorded outcome rather than a stale-designation rejection, and a mismatched-reuse fixture in which that committed designation identity is re-presented naming a different target certificate and is refused as a conflict, moving the projection nowhere and returning no stored outcome.
- F210-AC-07 includes a fixture in which a member of the owning workspace who holds no certificate read or download permission is refused a download URL while a permitted member is issued one, a fixture in which the permission is revoked between two issuances and the second is refused, and a fixture confirming that a URL issued before the revocation still resolves for its disclosed validity and that this bounded window is the documented limit of the control.
- F210-AC-10 includes a fixture in which an actor holding no membership of the owning workspace names a valid certificate and is refused at creation, at replacement and every field edit, at the designation action, and at every certificate, gap, and projection read, with a response that does not distinguish absence from denial, and a fixture in which authority removed while a mutation is in flight fails that request rather than committing.
- F210-AC-06 includes a fixture in which an actor holding an active membership of two workspaces creates a certificate in one while naming an F-201 insurance plan item and its F-202 checklist element from the other, and is refused in whole with no certificate version recorded and a response that does not distinguish absence from a different workspace, and a same-workspace control fixture in which the identical link is accepted and pinned.
- Security-sensitive and cross-workspace paths require negative authorization tests; provider paths require success, duplicate-delivery, retry, invalid-signature, and permanent-failure tests where applicable.
