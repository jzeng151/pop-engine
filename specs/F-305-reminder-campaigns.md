# F-305 · Reminder Campaigns

**Status:** PROPOSED (2026-07-26) — ready for review; not implementable until approved and listed in `docs/BASELINE.md`.

**Phase:** 2 · **Issue:** [#22](https://github.com/jzeng151/pop-engine/issues/22) · **Owner:** TBD · **Reviewer:** product owner · **Approval date:** —

## Purpose and User Outcome

An organizer can schedule consent-eligible RSVP reminders at T-7, T-1, and day-of, with preview, cancellation, suppression, and durable delivery.

## Scope

**In scope**

- Create channel-specific campaign drafts for the three Roadmap offsets, select eligible recipients, preview, schedule, cancel, and inspect delivery results.
- Reuse F-203 delivery plumbing through the approved durable job/outbox model.
- Enforce a distinct channel-specific transactional-notification lawful/consent basis, separate email/SMS marketing consent, and central purpose-scoped suppression before every attempt.

**Non-goals**

- Emergency messages, arbitrary marketing automation, segmentation beyond event RSVP eligibility, or contact acquisition.
- Sending to contacts without the required channel-specific transactional-notification basis.

## Dependencies and Baseline

- F-302 RSVPs, F-203 messaging plumbing, F-403 consent, approved Event Revisions, and the F-701/F-702/F-703 gate. F-702 supplies the workspace membership boundary the event and its campaigns resolve against and F-703 supplies the permission matrix `F305-AC-10` checks; F-701 supplies the authenticated actor both read from. F-701 is APPROVED (2026-07-28, `docs/BASELINE.md`); F-702 and F-703 remain PROPOSED, so the gate is not an approved input today and this spec is not implementable against it until those two are approved and listed in `docs/BASELINE.md`.
- Approved job/outbox, consent, timezone, provider, and template contracts.
- Baseline at draft time: PRD, Roadmap, Design, and Phase 0–1.5 Architecture approved 2026-07-22; `ARCHITECTURE-FUTURE.md` approved as a planning target 2026-07-25; NYC ruleset `nyc.v2.7`, rules schema `popengine-rules/v2`, and scenario fixtures v5 where regulatory output is consumed.
- Operand binding and replay ordering for client-supplied identities: `specs/F-411-staff-roles-credentialed-entry.md` F411-AC-08 states once, for this whole branch, that a client-supplied identity is committed with the operands that determine its recorded result and that a reuse carrying different operands is a conflict rather than a replay, and that a committed identity is resolved before any version, generation, state, or limit check whose answer the committed operation itself changed, while the acting actor's current authority is re-read at the replay and must still admit the operation before any stored outcome is returned. Every identity criterion below relies on it. F-411 is PROPOSED, so that rule is not an approved input today and this spec is not implementable against it until F-411 is approved or the rule is promoted to an approved shared invariant; F411-AC-08 records both paths.
- The approval PR must re-pin any baseline version that changes before approval. A proposed or superseded input blocks implementation.

## Inputs, Outputs, State, Validation, and Errors

- Inputs are an exact event revision and lifecycle generation, channel, approved template content, one Roadmap offset, and, on every draft mutation, the exact draft version it was composed against plus its own request identity under F305-AC-08; outputs are a recipient snapshot plus message jobs/attempts pinned to that revision, lifecycle generation, and exact channel-endpoint version.
- Campaign state is draft → scheduled → sending → completed, partially failed, cancelled, or failed; cancellation marks the send generation stale and prevents unsent work from reaching a provider.
- Eligibility, pinned revision, event lifecycle generation, campaign generation, RSVP status, and exact channel-endpoint version are rechecked immediately before provider delivery so later transactional opt-out, suppression, endpoint change, reschedule, event cancellation/archive, campaign cancellation, or RSVP cancellation wins over the schedule snapshot.
- Missing or unresolved material data stays visibly unset, unknown, pending, or failed as appropriate; it never becomes a successful or complete result.
- Invalid input produces a field or action-specific error without partial mutation. Retriable external failures preserve the user's confirmed state and expose a safe retry.

## UI and Accessibility

- Preview shows channel, send time/timezone, eligible/suppressed counts, exact copy, and a confirmation step before scheduling.
- The complete workflow is keyboard operable, uses programmatic labels and visible focus, does not encode status by color alone, and announces asynchronous success or failure.
- Empty, loading, permission-denied, validation, provider-failure, and unavailable states have explicit copy and a safe next action.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **F305-AC-01:** T-7, T-1, and day-of schedules resolve from the pinned event revision in the event timezone and reject a send time already invalid under the approved immediate-send policy; when a new revision changes any approved message-relevant field, one transaction marks the old send generation cancelled and schedules replacements pinned to the new revision, recomputing times when date/time changed and otherwise preserving them, while retaining sent attempts/history. That reconciliation transaction names the exact campaign state and send generation it read and compare-and-swaps both, so it and an organizer cancellation compete on the same row and exactly one wins. Without it the two are unordered and the cancellation loses silently: the revision transaction reads the campaign as `scheduled`, the cancellation commits, and the revision transaction then advances the send generation and creates replacement jobs for a campaign the organizer was told was cancelled. AC-05 does not prevent that, because it fences cancellation against delivery claims on jobs that already exist and says nothing about jobs created afterwards. A reconciliation that finds the campaign cancelled, or its generation moved, cancels nothing and creates no replacement; a cancellation that finds the generation moved is rejected and returns the current campaign, so the organizer cancels the replacements rather than being told a stale set was stopped.
2. **F305-AC-02:** Only RSVP contacts whose RSVP is currently active, and who carry the approved channel-specific transactional-notification lawful/consent record and no active suppression for that purpose, receive a job. An RSVP that F-302 has moved to `cancelled` is not an eligible recipient, at selection and at every recheck: the transactional basis and the endpoint survive the cancellation because they are properties of the contact, so a criterion that reads only those two admits a guest who is no longer attending. Marketing consent is not required; a marketing opt-out remains enforced for marketing without withdrawing the distinct transactional basis. A new RSVP, an RSVP cancellation, newly established transactional-notification basis, removed suppression, or channel-endpoint change advances the recipient generation and cancels that recipient's obsolete unsent jobs; each of the transitions that leaves the recipient eligible then creates exactly one replacement job for each still-pending campaign offset when the current endpoint remains eligible, and an RSVP cancellation creates none, because the recipient it would replace work for is no longer one. Replay or concurrent enrollment cannot duplicate a job, and an offset already invalid under the approved immediate-send policy remains unscheduled.
3. **F305-AC-03:** Delivery atomically claims a non-cancellable `sending` state after its final eligibility, generation, RSVP-status, and exact channel-endpoint-version check; transactional-notification withdrawal, suppression, endpoint change, reschedule, event cancellation/archive, campaign cancellation, or RSVP cancellation serializes against that claim and cannot report prevention for already-sending work. RSVP cancellation is one of the transitions this fence orders, not a state the fence may read past: F-302's cancellation leaves the RSVP record in place, so a job whose final check asks only about consent and endpoint finds both intact and delivers the T-1 or day-of reminder to a guest who cancelled. A job that fails the RSVP-status check is cancelled rather than delivered, and one that has already claimed `sending` remains accounted already-sending work on the same terms as every other transition here. Unrelated revisions do not stale the message generation.
4. **F305-AC-04:** Retries, worker crashes, and duplicate claims do not create more than one accepted provider delivery per recipient/campaign/channel.
5. **F305-AC-05:** Cancellation stops unclaimed and pre-`sending` leased jobs before provider delivery; a job that already claimed the non-cancellable `sending` state continues as accounted already-sending work under AC-03. Attempts/history remain preserved and sent, suppressed, failed, already-sending, and cancelled counts stay accurate.
   Organizer cancellation is a durable transition of the campaign and not only a fence over the delivery claim, so it names the exact campaign state and send generation it was composed against, commits only by compare-and-swap on both, which is the same pair F305-AC-01's reconciliation transaction competes on, and carries its own stable client-supplied request identity, distinct from the F305-AC-08 draft and F305-AC-09 scheduling identities, committed in that same transaction under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the campaign. A recognized replay returns the outcome the original cancellation recorded, including its sent, suppressed, failed, already-sending, and cancelled counts, and cancels nothing a second time. That identity binds its operands under the rule `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch: it is committed together with the request fields that determine the recorded result, including every aggregate, version, generation, and state the request names, and a later request presenting the same identity with any different operand is refused as a conflict rather than being answered with the stored result. That identity is resolved in the order `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch, applied here rather than restated: a request presenting an already committed identity is resolved from that record before the comparisons above, and returns the outcome that request originally recorded, because the commit the retry repeats is exactly what moved the state those comparisons read. The acting actor's current authority is not one of the checks that identity is resolved past: the authority that admits a first attempt is re-read server-side at the replay, and the stored outcome is returned only if it still admits the request, so a replay presented after that authority is gone is refused and discloses no more than a first request would; the only exception is authority the committed operation itself removed, which `F411-AC-08` states once for this branch. A cancellation whose identity resolves to no committed outcome is then held to every one of them, and one refused by any of them commits no identity, so re-presenting it later is a first cancellation and not a replay.

   Without it this criterion has only the comparisons, and a comparison cannot tell a retry from a stale request. F305-AC-08 identifies draft creation and every draft mutation and F305-AC-09 identifies scheduling, so cancellation is the one organizer transition on a campaign that already exists with nothing to resolve against. When a cancellation wins the race F305-AC-01 describes and its response is lost, the retry is composed against the pre-cancellation state and generation that its own commit advanced, so AC-01's rule that a cancellation finding the generation moved is rejected and returns the current campaign applies to the organizer's own retry: they are told a stale set was not stopped, or shown a campaign whose state they cannot reconcile with the action they took, at the point where the accounted counts this criterion promises are what they need. The plausible next action, cancelling again, is indistinguishable from a first cancellation of a campaign a reconciliation already cancelled, so it settles nothing either.

6. **F305-AC-06:** The day-of reminder includes confirmed directions pinned and linked to their source record/version. Missing or unconfirmed directions block that reminder, and a directions-source change invalidates and replaces unsent work under AC-01.
7. **F305-AC-07:** Scheduling rejects a cancelled or archived event. A transition to either state advances the lifecycle generation and atomically cancels every job that has not claimed `sending`; already-sending work remains accounted under AC-03 and no replacement reminder is scheduled.
8. **F305-AC-08:** Draft creation, not scheduling, is the transaction that creates the campaign, matching the `draft → scheduled` state model and the draft operations the API surface already carries. It takes a stable client-supplied request identity supplied before the campaign exists, and the transaction that creates the draft commits that identity under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the event; a recognized replay returns the original draft campaign and creates no second one, while a deliberately separate campaign uses a new identity. F305-AC-04's per `recipient/campaign/channel` guarantee does not reach this case, because two campaigns each deliver once to the same recipients and neither exceeds its own limit. That identity binds its operands under the rule `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch: it is committed together with the request fields that determine the recorded result, including every aggregate, version, and generation the request names, and a later request presenting the same identity with any different operand is refused as a conflict rather than being answered with the stored result. The identity is still the key and the operands are only a precondition on reusing it, so this is not the content uniqueness this criterion already forbids. That identity is resolved in the order `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch, applied here rather than restated: a request presenting an already committed identity is resolved from that record before the version, generation, state, and limit checks this criterion requires, and returns the outcome that request originally recorded, because the commit the retry repeats is exactly what moved the state those checks read. The acting actor's current authority is not among the checks the identity is resolved past: the authority this feature requires for a first attempt is re-read server-side at the replay, and the stored outcome is returned only if it still admits the operation, so a replay presented after that authority is gone is refused and discloses no more than a first request would; the only exception is authority the committed operation itself removed, which `F411-AC-08` states once for this branch. A request whose identity resolves to no committed outcome is then held to every one of them, and a request refused by one of them commits no identity, so re-presenting it later is a first attempt and not a replay.

   The draft campaign is a versioned aggregate from that creating transaction onward, and every later mutation of it names the exact draft version it was composed against and commits only by compare-and-swap on that version, carrying its own stable request identity distinct from the creation identity above, committed under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the campaign. That is every mutable part of the draft and not only the field a reviewer happens to name: channel, template content selection, the Roadmap offset, the recipient scope the preview is computed from, any other approved draft-held value, and the discard or deletion of the draft itself are each composed against a named version on the same terms. A stale mutation changes nothing, and returns the current draft for the organizer to reload, recompose, and preview again.

   F305-AC-09 compare-and-swaps the draft version at scheduling, which stops a schedule built from a draft the organizer did not preview and cannot recover an edit already lost inside the draft. Without the comparison above, two organizers editing one draft from a single observed version both report success and the later write silently replaces the earlier one's channel, content, or offset, so AC-09 then schedules a draft that is perfectly current and missing a change its organizer was told had saved, and the recipient snapshot and jobs it creates in the same transaction go out against that draft. Without the per-mutation identity, a lost response on a draft edit gives the organizer no way to tell a committed edit from a failed one, and the retry is a fresh mutation against a version the first attempt has already advanced.

9. **F305-AC-09:** Scheduling is a transition of a campaign that already exists. It names the exact draft campaign version the organizer previewed and confirmed, carries its own stable client-supplied request identity distinct from AC-08's, committed under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the campaign, and commits only by compare-and-swap on that version: success moves the campaign from `draft` to `scheduled` and creates the recipient snapshot and jobs in the same transaction, while a version mismatch schedules nothing, creates no snapshot or job, and returns the current draft for the organizer to preview and confirm again. Scheduling names and compare-and-swaps the Event revision and event lifecycle generation the preview read as well as the draft campaign version, and all three must still be current at commit. The draft version alone is not enough, and the case that shows it is one AC-01 already assumes cannot happen: an Event revision that commits between preview and scheduling finds no scheduled campaign to cancel or replace, so it does nothing, while the draft version it never touched is still current, so a comparison on that version alone succeeds and schedules the superseded revision's jobs. The delivery-time revision recheck then suppresses those jobs and no replacement pinned to the current revision was ever created, so reminders the organizer was shown as scheduled silently never send. Rejecting on a moved revision or generation returns the organizer to preview, where the campaign is rebuilt against the current revision and confirmed again, which is the same recovery a moved draft version already produces. A recognized replay of the scheduling identity returns the original scheduled result and creates no second recipient snapshot or job set. Splitting the two is what makes both implementable: binding scheduling to AC-08's creation identity would leave the transition itself with no replay or stale-write protection, while treating scheduling as the creation would leave the draft that the scope and state model require for preview with nowhere durable to live. That identity binds its operands under the rule `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch: it is committed together with the request fields that determine the recorded result, including every aggregate, version, and generation the request names, and a later request presenting the same identity with any different operand is refused as a conflict rather than being answered with the stored result. The identity is still the key and the operands are only a precondition on reusing it, so this is not the content uniqueness this criterion already forbids. That identity is resolved in the order `F411-AC-08` states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch, applied here rather than restated: a request presenting an already committed identity is resolved from that record before the version, generation, state, and limit checks this criterion requires, and returns the outcome that request originally recorded, because the commit the retry repeats is exactly what moved the state those checks read. The acting actor's current authority is not among the checks the identity is resolved past: the authority this feature requires for a first attempt is re-read server-side at the replay, and the stored outcome is returned only if it still admits the operation, so a replay presented after that authority is gone is refused and discloses no more than a first request would; the only exception is authority the committed operation itself removed, which `F411-AC-08` states once for this branch. A request whose identity resolves to no committed outcome is then held to every one of them, and a request refused by one of them commits no identity, so re-presenting it later is a first attempt and not a replay.

10. **F305-AC-10:** Every organizer-facing operation this feature defines names the campaign it acts on, or on draft creation the event the campaign will belong to, and the workspace that owns it, and is admitted only by the acting actor's current F-702 membership of that workspace together with the F-703 permission approved for the action, both re-read server-side from stored membership and role at the moment of the operation and, for a write, inside the same transaction that commits it. That covers draft creation and every draft mutation, including discard and deletion, under F305-AC-08, scheduling under F305-AC-09, organizer cancellation under F305-AC-01 and F305-AC-05, the preview with its eligible and suppressed counts and the recipient contact scope it is computed from under F305-AC-08 and the UI and Accessibility section, and every inspection of campaign status, attempts, and delivery results. A request failing the check is refused before any durable write and before any campaign field, recipient contact, eligibility or suppression count, or delivery result is disclosed; recipient contact data in particular is never read into a preview or result for a caller the check turns away. Its response does not distinguish an event or campaign that does not exist from one the actor may not see. The check is at the operation and not at session start or workspace switch, so authority removed while a request is in flight causes that request to fail rather than commit. Attendee-facing consent, opt-out, and suppression flows are not operations this spec defines; they belong to F-403 and F-302 and are bound by the consent and suppression records those specs state, not by this membership check.

Without this criterion AC-01 through AC-09 all pass for a caller who names another workspace's event or campaign. They fix schedule resolution, recipient eligibility, delivery claims, retry safety, cancellation fencing, directions pinning, lifecycle rejection, and the versioned draft and scheduling transitions, and not one of them asks who the organizer-side actor is. The surface that set leaves open reads another organizer's recipient contacts, suppression state, and delivery results through preview and status, and creates, edits, schedules, or cancels reminder campaigns that send to their attendees, for anyone who can name the event.

One input this criterion needs is not established by any approved artifact today and is not invented here. F-703 is PROPOSED and names no role set, so the permission above cannot be named. Until F-703 is approved this criterion is testable only as "every organizer-facing campaign creation, mutation, scheduling, cancellation, preview, and status read is refused unless the acting actor holds an active membership of the workspace that owns the named event, read server-side at that operation, and a refusal discloses nothing about whether that event or campaign exists", not against a named role or permission identifier. Naming the campaign creation, read, scheduling, and cancellation permissions with F-703 is an approval blocker below.

11. **F305-AC-11:** Every RSVP contact, consent and suppression record, and channel endpoint version selected into a preview, a recipient snapshot, or a job must resolve to the same event and the same workspace as the campaign, compared server-side at the operation that selects them and again inside the transaction that schedules, replaces, or claims the work they feed. F305-AC-02 admits a contact by the approved channel-specific transactional-notification basis and the absence of an active suppression for that purpose, which are properties of the contact and say nothing about which event the contact belongs to; F305-AC-10 admits the operation by the acting actor's current membership of the workspace that owns the campaign's event. An organizer who holds an active membership covering two events, whether in one workspace or two, satisfies both while scheduling a campaign for event B against RSVP contacts from event A: every contact carries a real transactional basis, no suppression applies, and every check the criteria state passes.

    The consequence reaches attendees and is not recoverable by refusing a later read. A's guests receive reminders naming B's date, time, and pinned directions under F305-AC-06, so the send is a disclosure of B's event details to people who never registered for it and a message about an event they cannot attend; F305-AC-03's non-cancellable `sending` claim means cancellation cannot stop the ones already claimed. Afterwards A's contacts appear in B's delivery results, attempts, and eligible and suppressed counts, so B's organizer reads A's attendee contact data through a status surface F305-AC-10 was written to protect. The same comparison governs the replacement jobs F305-AC-02 creates when a recipient generation advances, because a replacement selected from the wrong event repeats the send after the original is cancelled.

    The comparison runs along the chain and not to the event alone. A channel endpoint version, a consent record, and a suppression record are records of a contact, so each must resolve to the RSVP contact it is selected for and that contact to the campaign's event, rather than each being tied to the event on its own. Two contacts of one event both satisfy an event comparison, so without the contact link a snapshot can pair one recipient's identity with another's email address or phone number and the delivery-time recheck, which asks whether the contact is still eligible, still passes. The comparison is made at selection, inside the transaction that schedules or replaces the work, and again by each job immediately before the provider call, because a suppression or consent record can change between them.

    This applies `F702-AC-10`'s fourth obligation, which states once for the whole feature set that every record an operation names must resolve to one workspace, and it makes the comparison at the event as well as the workspace on the terms `F406-AC-09` and `F407-AC-08` state, because the campaign's aggregate is an event and one workspace holds many events, so a workspace comparison alone admits a sibling event's guest list. It is stated here because an implementation is built to this spec's criteria. Attendee-facing consent, opt-out, and suppression flows remain F-403's and F-302's, unchanged: this criterion adds where a contact must live to be selectable, not a new basis for contacting one. F-702 is `PROPOSED`, so the workspace a record resolves to is not an approved input today and this criterion is not fully implementable until F-702 is approved and listed in `docs/BASELINE.md`; the event half is testable against F-302's own event association today, and until F-702 is approved the workspace half is testable only as "no contact, consent record, suppression record, or endpoint version outside the campaign's own event is selected into a preview, snapshot, or job, and an attempt to schedule against one is refused with nothing scheduled and no contact disclosed", not against a named workspace model.

## Fixtures and Verification

- F305-AC-01 includes a concurrent fixture in which an Event revision reconciliation and an organizer cancellation race on one scheduled campaign and exactly one wins, proving that a winning cancellation leaves no replacement jobs behind.
- F305-AC-05 includes a fixture in which an organizer cancellation commits, its response is lost, and the retry presenting the same cancellation identity and the pre-cancellation campaign state and send generation returns the original recorded outcome and its accounted counts rather than a stale-generation rejection, cancelling nothing a second time, plus a mismatched-reuse fixture in which that committed identity is re-presented naming a different campaign and is refused as a conflict, cancelling nothing.
- F305-AC-02 and F305-AC-03 include a fixture in which an organizer cancels an RSVP after the campaign is scheduled and before any provider claim: the recipient generation advances, that recipient's unsent T-1 and day-of jobs are cancelled, no replacement job is created, and no provider call is made even though the transactional basis and the channel endpoint are both still intact. A paired fixture cancels the RSVP after that recipient's job has claimed the non-cancellable `sending` state and proves the job stays accounted as already-sending work rather than reporting prevention, and a control fixture confirms an RSVP that is not cancelled still receives both reminders.
- F305-AC-09 includes a fixture in which an Event revision commits between preview and scheduling, finds no scheduled campaign to replace, and the subsequent scheduling is rejected on the moved revision with no snapshot and no jobs created, rather than scheduling jobs the delivery-time recheck would suppress.
- Planned automated fixture IDs are the acceptance IDs above; each must map one-to-one to a runnable test before approval can claim implementation readiness.
- Regulatory fixtures: none; this feature does not define regulatory ground truth.
- F305-AC-10 includes a fixture in which an actor holding no membership of the owning workspace names a valid event and campaign and is refused at draft creation, at every draft mutation including discard, at scheduling, at cancellation, at preview, and at every status and delivery-result read, with a response that does not distinguish absence from denial and no recipient contact disclosed, a fixture in which authority removed while a scheduling request is in flight fails that request rather than committing, and a paired fixture confirming the attendee-facing consent and opt-out paths F-403 and F-302 own are unaffected.
- F305-AC-11 includes a fixture in which a campaign for one event is scheduled against RSVP contacts belonging to another event in the same workspace and is refused with no job created and no contact disclosed in the preview counts, an equivalent fixture across two workspaces, a fixture in which a recipient-generation advance produces replacement jobs and none of them selects a contact outside the campaign's event, and a same-event control fixture in which the identical schedule commits. It also includes a cross-contact fixture in which every contact resolves to the campaign's event but one recipient's snapshotted endpoint version, consent record, or suppression record belongs to a different contact of that same event: the recipient is refused at selection, and a paired fixture planting the same mismatch after scheduling proves the job issues no provider call.
- Security-sensitive and cross-workspace paths require negative authorization tests; provider paths require success, duplicate-delivery, retry, invalid-signature, and permanent-failure tests where applicable.
