# F-411 · Staff Roles and Credentialed Entry

**Status:** PROPOSED (2026-07-26) — ready for review; not implementable until approved and listed in `docs/BASELINE.md`.

**Phase:** 3 · **Issue:** [#43](https://github.com/jzeng151/pop-engine/issues/43) · **Owner:** TBD · **Reviewer:** product owner plus affected architecture, contract, security, and lane owners (TBD) · **Approval date:** —

## Purpose and User Outcome

Event staff can perform only their assigned door operations and validate attendee, vendor, or performer entry categories without receiving broader workspace access.

## Scope

**In scope**

- Event-scoped staff assignments with check-in/entry permissions and credential categories for attendee, vendor, and performer.
- Issue, revoke, and validate opaque event credentials; record accepted/denied attempts.
- Enforce F-703 server-side authorization at every door action.

**Non-goals**

- Background checks, identity verification, physical badge printing, payroll, scheduling, access-control hardware, or arbitrary policy scripting.
- Using a credential as marketing consent or regulatory evidence.

## Dependencies and Baseline

- F-703, F-401, and F-410 for directional entry where used, and the full F-701/F-702/F-703 gate for the organizer-side surfaces. F-702 supplies the workspace membership boundary the event and its staff assignments, credentials, and attempt history resolve against and F-703 supplies the permission matrix `F411-AC-09` checks; F-701 supplies the authenticated actor both read from. No earlier revision of this spec declared F-701 or F-702, which left the organizer-side criteria reading a membership their own dependency list did not name. All three are PROPOSED, so the gate is not an approved input today and this spec is not implementable against it until they are approved and listed in `docs/BASELINE.md`.
- Approved credential token, expiry, revocation, category, and staff permission contracts.
- Baseline at draft time: PRD, Roadmap, Design, and Phase 0–1.5 Architecture approved 2026-07-22; `ARCHITECTURE-FUTURE.md` approved as a planning target 2026-07-25; NYC ruleset `nyc.v2.7`, rules schema `popengine-rules/v2`, and scenario fixtures v5 where regulatory output is consumed.
- The approval PR must re-pin any baseline version that changes before approval. A proposed or superseded input blocks implementation.

## Inputs, Outputs, State, Validation, and Errors

- Inputs are an exact authorized staff-assignment generation and event-credential version, plus, on issuance, the subject, category, expiry, and a stable request identity; every validation additionally carries its own client-generated operation identity under F411-AC-08; outputs are the issued credential, minimal validation result, and append-only attempt/entry record.
- Credential state is active → revoked or expired; validation is event-bound and reveals only the minimum door decision.
- Unknown/malformed/wrong-event credentials deny without disclosing private attendee/vendor data.
- Missing or unresolved material data stays visibly unset, unknown, pending, or failed as appropriate; it never becomes a successful or complete result.
- Invalid input produces a field or action-specific error without partial mutation. Retriable external failures preserve the user's confirmed state and expose a safe retry.

## UI and Accessibility

- Door UI identifies active event/staff role, gives non-color accept/deny feedback, and supports scanner/keyboard fallback.
- The complete workflow is keyboard operable, uses programmatic labels and visible focus, does not encode status by color alone, and announces asynchronous success or failure.
- Empty, loading, permission-denied, validation, provider-failure, and unavailable states have explicit copy and a safe next action.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. **F411-AC-01:** A check-in staff member can perform only the approved event-scoped door actions and no broader organizer/admin operation.
2. **F411-AC-02:** Validation and its accepted attempt/entry record serialize against both credential revocation and staff-assignment removal on the exact versions, so exactly one wins: a winning validation records one idempotent entry and returns the approved minimal category/decision, while a winning revocation or removal prevents acceptance.
3. **F411-AC-03:** Revoked, expired, malformed, guessed, or wrong-event credentials deny without revealing identity/contact details and append one idempotent non-sensitive attempt with the denial category.
4. **F411-AC-04:** Vendor and performer categories remain distinct from attendee and do not create RSVP, consent, or regulatory status.
5. **F411-AC-05:** Removing a staff assignment advances its generation and serializes against validation's claim of the prior generation; a winning removal blocks that validation and every later protected action, while preserving prior attempt attribution.
6. **F411-AC-06:** Issuance is an acceptance criterion, not a setup step: only an actor holding the approved issuing permission for that exact event can issue a credential, and the issuing transaction binds in one commit the event, the subject the credential admits, exactly one category from the approved category list, the expiry instant, the initial active state, and an opaque high-entropy token that no other issued credential holds. An issuance missing any of those bindings is rejected and creates nothing, so F411-AC-02, F411-AC-03, and F411-AC-04 can rely on every stored credential carrying them and the door feature is usable without seeded credentials. The issue request carries a client-generated request identity committed in that same transaction under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the event, matching the scope F411-AC-08 uses for a validation, so a retry after a lost response returns the original credential rather than issuing a second live token for the same subject; a deliberate second credential sends a new identity. The retry is admitted by the issuing permission for that exact event on the same terms as the first attempt, re-read at that request under F411-AC-09 rather than resolved past, because the issuance did not change the issuer's authority; an issuer whose permission or workspace membership is gone is refused and receives no stored credential. That identity binds its operands under F411-AC-08: the event, the subject, the category, and the expiry instant committed with it. A reuse presenting any different operand is refused as a conflict and issues nothing, rather than returning a credential that was issued for a different subject or category. Uniqueness over subject and category is not that enforcement: one subject may legitimately hold two credentials, so it would refuse a real issuance.
7. **F411-AC-07:** Credential validation is rate limited per event and per validating staff assignment. Within that limit every validation denies or accepts normally, appends its non-sensitive attempt record under F411-AC-03, and is visibly reported to the door operator rather than silently dropped. Once the limit is exceeded for that key, further requests are refused before any per-request durable write, including before the F411-AC-08 identity row, and the evidence of that refused activity is one coalesced record per event, staff assignment, and window, carrying the first and last refused instant and a count of refusals. That record is written at most once per key per approved flush interval, so the durable writes a refused flood produces are bounded by the number of windows and flush intervals it spans and not by the number of requests it sends; refusals arriving between flushes are answered from the limiter's own non-durable state and update no row. Between flushes the persisted last-refused instant and count are therefore accurate as of the last flush and are read as a lower bound on refusals to date, which the record states rather than presenting as a total. Appending one attempt record per refused request would make the limiter an amplifier, and updating the coalesced record in place on every refused request keeps that amplification while only bounding the row count: the cheap path a compromised door device or a stolen staff assignment controls is exactly the path that persists, so denied validation still buys the caller unbounded event-day write load. The refusal is still reported to the operator with a safe next action, and the coalesced record is still queryable evidence that the limit was reached, so bounding the write does not cost the audit trail its meaning. The exact limit and window are not approved today: they belong to the credential lifecycle and token threat model named in the Approval Blockers, which must also name the coalescing window, the flush interval, and the retention bound on coalesced records, because an unbounded set of coalescing keys reintroduces the growth this bound was added to stop. Until that approval names them, this criterion is testable only as "a configured finite limit is enforced, requests over it deny, and both the number of durable rows and the number of durable writes they produce are bounded by a finite count per key, window, and flush interval rather than growing with the request count," not against a specific number. This is stated here rather than only under Privacy and security because a repository agent implements acceptance criteria, and an unenforced limit leaves the opaque token guessable at whatever rate the door endpoint answers.

   Validation is the only operation this feature defines that an unauthorized or compromised caller can drive at will, which is why the bound is placed here and not repeated elsewhere. F411-AC-06 issuance, credential revocation, and F411-AC-05 staff-assignment removal each require the approved permission for that exact event under F411-AC-01, and a request failing that check is refused before any durable write, so none of them converts a rejected request into a persisted row.

8. **F411-AC-08:** Every validation carries a client-generated operation identity supplied by the door client, committed under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the event together with that validation's accepted or denied outcome. A replay presenting the same identity returns the stored outcome, appends no second attempt or entry record, and consumes no second unit of the F411-AC-07 limit; a genuinely separate presentation of the same credential sends a new identity and is validated normally. This is operation identity, never content uniqueness: the credential, the staff assignment, and the submitted payload may not serve as the key, because a deliberate rescan of one credential and a repeated denied guess are both legitimate, and F411-AC-03's attempt history and F411-AC-07's rate limiting are only meaningful while those repeats stay distinct. A server-generated request identifier is not that enforcement either, because a retry after a lost response carries a new one and duplicates the attempt it was meant to collapse.

   F411-AC-02 and F411-AC-03 each require an idempotent record, and the only stable request identity this spec otherwise defines belongs to F411-AC-06 issuance, so nothing gives a validation an identity to be idempotent on. When a validation commits its attempt or entry and the response is lost, the operator rescans and one presentation becomes two records: an accepted entry attributed twice, or a denial charged twice against a limit whose size the threat model has not yet named.

   **A committed identity binds its operands, and a reuse that does not match them is a conflict and not a replay. This rule governs every client-supplied identity on this branch, not validation alone.** The identity is committed together with the operands that determine the operation's result, and for a validation those are the event, the presented credential, the validating staff assignment, and the submitted validation payload. A request presenting a committed identity is a replay, and receives the stored outcome, only when every one of those operands equals what was stored with it. When any of them differs, the request is refused as a conflict: it receives no stored outcome, commits nothing, appends no attempt or entry record, and is neither treated as a replay nor allowed to proceed as a fresh operation under that identity. The refusal names the conflict without disclosing the stored operation's credential, subject, or outcome, per F411-AC-03's non-disclosure rule, and the caller recovers by sending a new identity for the operation it actually intends.

   This is not the content uniqueness the paragraph above forbids, and the difference is which side of the commit each one sits on. Content uniqueness would make the payload the key, so two legitimate rescans of one credential would collapse into one record; that stays forbidden. Operand binding leaves the client-chosen identity as the key and makes the operands a precondition on reusing it, so two legitimate rescans still send two identities and produce two records, while one identity can only ever mean one operation. Without it, the identity is a replay key the client controls and the server honors for content it never computed the outcome against: a compromised or malfunctioning door client reuses an accepted identity with a different credential, receives an acceptance that was computed for the original credential, and no attempt or entry is recorded for the credential actually presented. Returning a stored outcome for different operands is not idempotency; it is a way to make one operation masquerade as another, and it holds for every identity a client chooses rather than only for this one.

   **This rule is not an approved shared contract today, and stating it in a PROPOSED Phase 3 spec does not make it one.** Every spec on this branch that carries this rule by reference names F-411 under its Dependencies and Baseline, so the reference is a declared dependency rather than an assumption. Two specs cannot carry it by reference and do not: this spec declares F-702 and F-703 as dependencies, because `F411-AC-09` is admitted by the workspace membership F-702 defines and consults the permission matrix F-703 defines, so a dependency in the other direction would make each pair blockers of each other and neither could be approved first. `F703-AC-03` and `F702-AC-12` therefore state this rule locally, on the reasoning their own Dependencies sections give, and `F702-AC-11` M-07, `F702-AC-08` T-11, and `F702-AC-08` T-12 apply F-702's local statement rather than this one. That dependency is real and it binds the approval order: a spec carrying it may not be approved and implemented while this criterion is unapproved, because an implementer confined to an approved spec would otherwise read an identity criterion whose operand binding lives in a PROPOSED artifact and, under AGENTS.md's rule that PROPOSED inputs stop the work, would either stop or implement the identity without the binding. Either outcome is worse than saying so here.

   Two things close it, and this spec has authority over neither. Approving F-411 makes this criterion an approved input the referencing specs may consume, which is the cheaper path and the one their dependency rows are written for. Promoting the rule to an approved shared invariant — an ADR or an `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` invariant covering every client-supplied identity in the system — removes the ordering constraint entirely and is the better long-term shape, because the rule is not about door credentials and does not belong to a door-credential spec. That promotion is a durable architecture decision under `docs/DOCUMENTATION-GOVERNANCE.md` §6 and needs its own ADR approval, so this branch records it as the option rather than taking it. Until one of the two happens, no spec on this branch is implementable against this rule, which is already true of all of them for other reasons: every one is PROPOSED.

   A conflict refused this way writes nothing durable per request, so it does not reintroduce the amplification F411-AC-07 removes; the evidence that mismatched reuse occurred is bounded and coalesced on the same terms as that criterion's refusal evidence.

   This criterion and F411-AC-07 are evaluated in one stated order, because each otherwise assumes it owns the request. A presented identity that already holds a committed outcome is resolved from that record first: if its operands match it returns the stored outcome, appending nothing and consuming no unit of the limit, and if they do not it is refused as a conflict, committing nothing. Every other validation is then checked against the F411-AC-07 limit and refused if it is exceeded, before its identity is committed. Only a request passing both is validated and commits its outcome and identity in one transaction. Resolving the identity first is what makes the two composable: checking the limit first would charge the operator's retry against the unit the original presentation already paid and could deny an entry that was already accepted, while committing an identity for a refused request would restore exactly the per-request durable write F411-AC-07 removes. The consequence is stated rather than left implied: a request refused over the limit stores no outcome for its identity, so resending that identity after the window has passed is a first attempt and not a replay, and this criterion's replay guarantee covers exactly the identities that reached a committed outcome.

   **A committed identity is resolved before any check whose answer the committed operation itself changed. This ordering rule governs every client-supplied identity on this branch, not this criterion's composition with F411-AC-07 alone.** The order stated above is one instance of a rule that holds wherever a replay check and a state check meet on one request. The identity is looked up first, against the record the committing transaction wrote; only a request whose identity resolves to no committed outcome is then evaluated against the version, generation, lifecycle-state, or rate-limit checks that guard a first attempt, and only a request passing those is admitted and commits its outcome and identity together. A state check made first cannot tell a retry from a fresh request, because the commit the retry is repeating is exactly what moved the state that check reads: the version it names has been advanced by that commit, the generation it claims has been superseded, the pending state it requires has become terminal, the credential that commit itself revoked is inactive, or the limit unit it needs was consumed by the original attempt. Every one of those rejects work that in fact succeeded and reports a completed operation to its caller as stale, conflicting, or invalid.

   **The acting actor's current authority is not one of the checks a committed identity is resolved past. It is re-read before any stored outcome is returned.** Membership, role, and permission are not state the commit moved: an ordinary write, a budget edit or a source-record creation or a staff-assignment modification, leaves the actor's authority exactly as it found it, so the check that guards its first attempt is equally able to judge its retry, and there is nothing about the retry that the check cannot tell from a fresh request. A replay is an operation, and every criterion on this branch that requires authority to be re-read server-side at the moment of the operation therefore reaches it: the actor presenting a committed identity is admitted only by the authority that criterion requires for a first attempt, re-read at that request, and the stored outcome is disclosed only if it still admits the operation. A replay presented after that authority is gone is refused on the terms the refusal already has, disclosing no more than a first request would about whether the record or the stored operation exists.

   Without that separation the replay contract silently outranks the authority contract, and it does so on exactly the surfaces the authority contract was added to close. `F411-AC-09` and its counterpart in every spec on this branch require authority to be re-read at the operation precisely so that authority removed while a request is in flight causes that request to fail, and each states that a refusal discloses nothing. A revoked actor who holds an identity their own earlier request committed, or a compromised client holding one, resends it and receives the result the current check would refuse; the surface that denies them a fresh read hands the same content back as a retry, and the disclosure the refusal was written to prevent happens anyway. Narrowing the ordering rule to the checks the commit actually moved is what keeps both contracts true at once, rather than choosing between answering a legitimate retry and refusing a revoked one.

   The one exception is authority the committed operation itself removed, and it is the case the ordering rule exists for. Where the commit being replayed is what ended the acting actor's own membership, assignment, or grant, that commit satisfies the check rather than failing it, and the replay returns the recorded outcome; the check is still made, and it is the commit's own effect that answers it. `F702-AC-11` M-07 is the named instance on this branch, a self-leave under M-06 whose replay an active-membership precheck would otherwise deny, and `F703-AC-03` states the same for a mutation that ends the acting actor's own grant. An operation whose commit did not remove the acting actor's own authority has no exception, which is every operation this spec defines: F411-AC-06 issuance, F411-AC-08 validation, and the F411-AC-09 mutations all leave the issuing or validating actor's authority untouched, so each of their replays is held to the current check.

   Resolving the identity first weakens none of the state checks. A resolved identity returns exactly the outcome that was already computed under all of them and is returned only after the current-authority re-read above, so it grants nothing the actor may not have at the moment of the replay; an unresolved identity is admitted only by passing every one of them; and a resolved identity whose operands do not match is refused as a conflict under the rule above rather than admitted to either path. The lookup is a read of a committed record and writes nothing per request, so it does not become the amplifier `F715-AC-09` bounds. The rule is directional and the consequence in the other direction is stated rather than left implied: a request refused by one of those checks commits no identity, so re-presenting it later is a first attempt and not a replay, and every replay guarantee on this branch covers exactly the identities that reached a committed outcome.

   This ordering, and the current-authority recheck that bounds it, are stated here once, beside the operand binding they compose with, and every criterion on this branch that pairs a client-supplied identity with a version, generation, state, authority, or limit check applies them by reference rather than restating them, so there is one formulation to change if the order is ever wrong. `F212-AC-04` states it for a disconnect whose replay a generation fence would refuse, `F307-AC-07` and `F307-AC-09` for draft and answer-set writes, `F603-AC-07` for a redelivery the limiter would charge, and `F306-AC-08`, `F403-AC-09` and `F715-AC-09` for the public surfaces whose rate limit would refuse a retry the original request already paid for. The same approval constraint the paragraph above records applies to this rule: it is not an approved shared contract today, and a spec carrying it by reference is not implementable against it until F-411 is approved or the rule is promoted to an approved shared invariant.

9. **F411-AC-09:** Every organizer-side operation this feature defines names the event it acts in and the workspace that owns it, and is admitted only by the acting actor's current F-702 membership of that workspace together with the F-703 permission approved for the action, both re-read server-side from stored membership and role at the moment of the operation and, for a write, inside the same transaction that commits it. The existing partial gates remain the rule for their legs and this criterion composes with them by name rather than restating them: F411-AC-06 keeps the approved issuing permission on issuance, F411-AC-02 and F411-AC-05 keep validation serialized against credential revocation and staff-assignment removal on exact versions, and F411-AC-07's aside keeps issuance, revocation, and assignment removal refused before any durable write when the approved permission is absent. This criterion covers what none of them gates: creating or modifying a staff assignment, credential revocation as an operation in its own right rather than as the thing validation serializes against, and reading the attempt/entry history, the issued-credential list, and the staff-assignment list. A request failing the check is refused before any durable write and before any assignment, credential, subject, or attempt record is disclosed, and its response does not distinguish an event, assignment, or credential that does not exist from one the actor may not see. The check is at the operation and not at session start or workspace switch, so authority removed while a request is in flight causes that request to fail rather than commit.

   Two surfaces are deliberately outside this criterion and stay outside it. Door-side credential validation is gated by the validating staff assignment under F411-AC-02 and F411-AC-05, whose exact-version serialization is that surface's currency check, not by a workspace membership the door client does not carry. Attendee, vendor, and performer credentials are opaque bearer tokens by design, bound by their event and time binding, revocation, and the F411-AC-07 limit rather than by any account. Without this criterion the remaining organizer surface is open on both sides: AC-01 names the approved door actions but no criterion gates who may create the staff assignment that confers them, so an authenticated caller who names another workspace's event could grant themselves or others a door role, revoke another organizer's credentials outside a validation race, and read the attempt history and credential and assignment lists that carry subjects and attribution, while every existing criterion passed.

   Each durable mutation this criterion covers also binds its own stable client-supplied request identity, committed in the same transaction as the change it identifies, under a uniqueness constraint scoped to the event, matching the scope F411-AC-06 uses for an issuance and F411-AC-08 for a validation. That is staff-assignment creation, every later modification of an assignment's permissions or credential categories, staff-assignment removal under F411-AC-05, and credential revocation as an operation in its own right. Each identity is distinct from the creation identity and from every other mutation's, and binds its operands under F411-AC-08: the event, the assignment or credential the request names, and the change it asks for, so a reuse naming a different assignment or credential is refused as a conflict and changes nothing. A presented identity that already holds a committed outcome is resolved from that record before the generation and version comparisons those criteria require, on the ordering F411-AC-08 states once for every client-supplied identity on this branch, and returns the outcome that request originally recorded: an assignment already removed is reported removed rather than absent, and a credential already revoked is reported revoked rather than inactive or unknown. The membership and permission re-read above is not resolved past: none of these mutations changes the acting actor's own authority, so that re-read is made at the replay as it is at a first attempt, and a retry presented after the actor's membership or permission is gone is refused on this criterion's terms and returns no stored outcome, rather than reporting an assignment or credential the actor may no longer see.

   Without the identity these mutations have only the comparisons, and a comparison cannot tell a retry from a stale or repeated request. F411-AC-05 removal advances the assignment's generation, so the retry names the generation the organizer read, which its own commit superseded, and is rejected as stale for a removal that already succeeded. A revocation retry is indistinguishable from a fresh revocation of a credential that is already revoked, so the organizer is told the credential was not active rather than that their revocation stands, and cannot tell an accepted revocation from a lost one at the moment they most need to. F411-AC-06 gave issuance a retry identity and F411-AC-08 gave validation one, and the mutations on the other side of the same lifecycle were left with none.

   One input this criterion needs is not established by any approved artifact today and is not invented here. F-703 is PROPOSED and names no role set, so the permission above cannot be named. Until F-703 is approved this criterion is testable only as "every staff-assignment write, credential revocation, and organizer-side list or history read is refused unless the acting actor holds an active membership of the workspace that owns the named event, read server-side at that operation, and a refusal discloses nothing about whether that event, assignment, or credential exists", not against a named role or permission identifier. Naming the assignment-management, revocation, and organizer read permissions with F-703 is an approval blocker below.

## Fixtures and Verification

- Planned automated fixture IDs are the acceptance IDs above; each must map one-to-one to a runnable test before approval can claim implementation readiness.
- F411-AC-08 includes a matched-replay fixture proving the stored outcome is returned with no second record and no second limit unit, and a mismatched-reuse fixture in which a committed identity is re-presented with a different credential and is refused as a conflict, returning no stored outcome, recording no attempt or entry for either credential, and disclosing nothing about the stored operation. F411-AC-06 includes the same mismatched-reuse fixture for issuance, in which a committed issue identity re-presented with a different subject issues nothing and returns no credential.
- F411-AC-09 includes a fixture in which an authenticated actor holding no membership of the owning workspace names a valid event and is refused at staff-assignment creation and modification, at credential revocation, and at the attempt-history, credential-list, and assignment-list reads, with a response that does not distinguish absence from denial, and a fixture in which authority removed while an assignment write or revocation is in flight fails that request rather than committing it. A paired fixture confirms door-side validation under a live staff assignment is unaffected. It also includes, for each of staff-assignment creation, modification, removal, and credential revocation, a fixture in which the mutation commits, its response is lost, and the retry presenting the same request identity and the pre-mutation generation or version returns the original recorded outcome rather than a stale-generation, already-removed, or already-revoked rejection, changing nothing a second time, plus a mismatched-reuse fixture in which a committed removal identity is re-presented naming a different staff assignment and is refused as a conflict, removing nobody. It also includes a revoked-replay fixture, for the recheck F411-AC-08 states: a staff-assignment write or credential revocation commits, its response is lost, the acting actor's membership of the owning workspace is then removed, and the retry presenting the same committed identity is refused and returns no stored outcome, with a response that does not distinguish absence from denial.
- Regulatory fixtures: none; this feature does not define regulatory ground truth.
- Security-sensitive and cross-workspace paths require negative authorization tests; provider paths require success, duplicate-delivery, retry, invalid-signature, and permanent-failure tests where applicable.
